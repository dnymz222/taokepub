170P/Christensen, also known as Comet Christensen 4, is a Jupiter-family comet with an 8.58-year orbit around the Sun. It is one of several comets discovered by American astronomer, Eric J. Christensen.

Observational history

Eric J. Christensen discovered a new comet from photographic plates taken from the 1.5-m reflector telescope that is used by the Mount Lemmon Survey (MLS) on 17 June 2005. At the time of discovery, the comet had an apparent magnitude of 19.9 and a condensed, circular coma about 15 arcseconds in length. Additional astrometry later determined that it was a short-periodic comet with an 8.64-year orbit around the Sun. It later received its official numerical designation (170P) alongside 169P/NEAT on 29 August 2005.

Orbit

As of November 2025, Comet 170P/Christensen revolves around the Sun once every 8.58 years, with an orbital inclination of about 10.13 degrees from the ecliptic. Its distance from the Sun varies from 2.911 AU (435.5 million km) and 5.472 AU (818.6 million km). Prior to discovery, the comet made its closest approach to Jupiter twice in 1921 and 1992, passing by the giant planet at a distance of 0.34 AU (51 million km) and 0.426 AU (63.7 million km) respectively.

Physical characteristics

Infrared observations from NEOWISE give a rough estimate of the size of its nucleus to be at least 23.77 km (14.77 mi) in diameter. However, it is likely that the comet is smaller due to the ongoing activity at the time its size was measured by the telescope. Extensive observations from the Asteroid Terrestrial-impact Last Alert System (ATLAS) survey between July 2022 and March 2024 showed that the comet reached its maximum dust production rate (≤ 34 kg/sec-1) roughly more than 200 days after reaching perihelion on 19 April 2023.
