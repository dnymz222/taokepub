NGC 2721 is a barred spiral galaxy in the constellation of Hydra. Its velocity with respect to the cosmic microwave background is 4,028±22 km/s, which corresponds to a Hubble distance of 193.8 ± 13.6 Mly (59.41 ± 4.17 Mpc). Additionally, 13 non-redshift measurements give a similar mean distance of 190.90 ± 7.29 Mly (58.531 ± 2.235 Mpc). It was discovered by German-British astronomer William Herschel on 1 February 1786. NGC 2721 is a Seyfert II galaxy, i.e. it has a quasar-like nucleus with very high surface brightnesses whose spectra reveal strong, high-ionisation emission lines, but unlike quasars, the host galaxy is clearly detectable.
