Xi Cygni is a binary star system in the constellation Cygnus. Its name is a Bayer designation that is Latinized from ξ Cygni and abbreviated Xi Cyg or ξ Cyg. The pair have a combined apparent visual magnitude of 3.73, making it readily visible to the naked eye as a point of light. Based on parallax measurements, the system is located at a distance of 350 parsecs (1,100 ly). At this distance, the apparent magnitude is diminished by 0.16 magnitudes from interstellar dust. This spectroscopic binary system contains two stars which orbit every 18 years in a moderately eccentric orbit. The primary star is a supergiant with a spectral type of around K4, while the secondary is an A-type main-sequence star with a spectral type of A1.5. Stellar winds from the supergiant have been measured at around 50 km/s, but with variations in speed and individual line strengths. ξ Cygni is in the Kepler spacecraft's field of view but no planets have been detected.
