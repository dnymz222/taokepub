Omega2 Cygni is a solitary star in the northern constellation of Cygnus. Its name is a Bayer designation that is Latinized from ω2 Cygni and abbreviated Omega2 Cyg or ω2 Cyg. This star has an apparent visual magnitude of 5.5, which is faintly visible to the naked eye on a dark night. Based upon an annual parallax shift of 7.18 mas, it is located approximately 454 light years from the Sun. At that distance, the visual magnitude is diminished by an extinction factor of 0.08 due to interstellar dust. This is a red giant star on the asymptotic giant branch, with a stellar classification of M2 III. Having exhausted the supply of hydrogen at its core, it has expanded to 45 times the Sun's radius. The star has 1.4 times the mass of the Sun, and is radiating 398 times the Sun's luminosity from its enlarged photosphere at an effective temperature of 3,920 K. It is a suspected variable star, although the evidence is considered "doubtful or erroneous". If it does exist, the variability is small with an amplitude of 0.05 magnitude and a timescale of around 30 days. There is a 58.3% chance that this star is a member of the Hercules stream.
