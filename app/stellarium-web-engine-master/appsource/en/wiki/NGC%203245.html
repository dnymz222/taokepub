NGC 3245 is a lenticular galaxy in the constellation Leo Minor. It was discovered by German-British astronomer William Herschel on April 11, 1785. The galaxy has an apparent visual magnitude of 10.7 and spans an angular size of 3.2′ × 1.8′. It is located at a distance of 68 million light-years (20.9 Mpc). The morphological classification of NGC 3245 is SA(r)0^0^, which is consistent with a lenticular galaxy (SA0) possessing a ring-like structure (r). There is a circumnuclear dust with a radius of 1.1″, which corresponds to a radius of 360 ly (110 pc). The rotational motion of this disk is consistent with a supermassive black hole at the core with a mass of (2.1±0.5)×108 M☉, or 210 million times the mass of the Sun. This is a member of the NGC 3254 Group of galaxies, which is a member of the Leo II Groups, a series of galaxies and galaxy clusters strung out from the right edge of the Virgo Supercluster.
