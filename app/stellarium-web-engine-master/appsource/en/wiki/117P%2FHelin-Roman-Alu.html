117P/Helin–Roman–Alu, also known as Helin–Roman–Alu 1, is a Jupiter-family comet with an 8.38-year orbit around the Sun. It is also classified as a quasi-Hilda comet. It is the second of three comets co-discovered by Eleanor F. Helin, Brian P. Roman, and Jeff Alu.

Physical characteristics

Preliminary estimates by James V. Scotti in 1994 revealed that the nucleus of Comet Helin–Roman–Alu 1 is roughly 9.0 km (5.6 mi) in diameter. Later estimates in 2005 revealed a much larger nucleus at approximately 32.8 km (20.4 mi), though it is highly likely that the strong activity seen from the comet at the time were contaminating the data obtained.
