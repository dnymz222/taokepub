NGC 4150 is a lenticular galaxy in the constellation of Coma Berenices. It was discovered by German-British astronomer William Herschel on March 13, 1785. This galaxy is located at a distance of 42.6 million light-years (13.06 Mpc) from the Milky Way. It has an apparent visual magnitude of 11.64 and spans an angular size of 2.3′ × 1.6′. The morphological classification of NGC 4150 is SA00(r)?, indicating this is a lenticular galaxy with possible ring structure. The galaxy is inclined at an angle of 58° with the long axis aligned along a position angle of 148°. It has a significant reservoir of molecular hydrogen that has a mass in the range (3.8 to 6.6)×107 M☉. The core region is kinematically decoupled from the rest of the galaxy, suggesting a recent merger. This minor merger added around 3×108 M☉ to the total mass.
