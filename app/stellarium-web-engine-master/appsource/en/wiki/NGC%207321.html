NGC 7321 is a barred spiral galaxy in the constellation of Pegasus. Its velocity with respect to the cosmic microwave background is 6,793±25 km/s, which corresponds to a Hubble distance of 326.8 ± 22.9 Mly (100.19 ± 7.02 Mpc). However, seven non-redshift measurements give a closer mean distance of 297.50 ± 14.14 Mly (91.214 ± 4.334 Mpc). It was discovered by German-British astronomer William Herschel on 17 November 1784. NGC 7321 is a LINER galaxy, i.e. a galaxy whose nucleus has an emission spectrum characterized by broad lines of weakly ionized atoms.

Galaxy group

According to M. J. Geller and J. P. Huchra, NGC 7321 is a member of a galaxy group which contains two other galaxies: NGC 7316 and NGC 7323.

Supernovae

Two supernovae have been observed in NGC 7321:

SN 2008gj (Type Ic, mag. 18.2) was discovered by the Lick Observatory Supernova Search (LOSS) on 19 October 2008. SN 2013di (Type Ia, mag. 18.2) was discovered by the Catalina Real-time Transient Survey on 12 June 2013.
